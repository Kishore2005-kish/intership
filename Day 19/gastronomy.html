Food that was
grown at altitude

One valley,
one harvest

Barley, black peas, potatoes and apricots. A single short summer produces everything the winter must carry. Nothing here is imported for effect.

Cooked by
the household

Every meal is made by the family hosting you, in the room where they eat. There is no menu card and no separate table for guests.

At the table

  • ThukpaHand-pulled noodles in a long-simmered bone or vegetable broth, with local greens
  • ThenthukTorn dough soup — the winter dish, eaten straight from the pot
  • Momos, steamedBuckwheat wrappers, potato and cheese from Kibber, chilli-tomato chutney
  • TsampaRoasted barley flour, kneaded with butter tea at the start of a walking day
  • Gur Gur ChaButter tea churned in a wooden cylinder, salted rather than sweet
  • ChhangFermented barley beer, served warm and sparingly, at the household's discretion
  • SeabuckthornWild berries from the riverbed, pressed into a sharp amber juice
  • Khambir with apricotThick fermented bread, dried apricots and walnuts from the Kinnaur road

Vegetarian
by default

Most of the valley eats meat rarely and in winter. Our table follows the valley, not the guest list. Vegan and gluten-free needs are met with what grows here, told to us in advance.

A note on
altitude

Appetite thins above twelve thousand feet. Eat lightly for the first two days, drink far more water than feels reasonable, and let the butter tea do the rest.
