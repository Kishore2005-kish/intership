Care guide

Care that suits Indian weather

Harsh summers, heavy monsoon and dry winter air all ask for different habits. Here is the short version our growers follow.

Four habits

Do these well and most plants forgive the rest.

Read the light

Bright but indirect means a spot where you can read comfortably at noon without the sun touching the leaves.

Water the soil, not the calendar

Push a finger two inches in. Dry means water thoroughly until it drains; damp means wait a day or two.

Let roots breathe

Use a loose mix of garden soil, cocopeat and compost, and always keep a drainage hole open.

Feed lightly

A spoon of compost once a month through the growing season beats a heavy dose once a year.

The monsoon note

During heavy rain, move pots under shelter, stop feeding, and skip watering entirely for indoor plants. Wipe leaves once a week so the humidity does not invite fungus.

Season by season

What to change through the year

Summer

Water early morning, shift pots away from hot glass, and mist leaves on the driest days.

Monsoon

Cut watering right back, check for waterlogging, and repot only if roots are circling the pot.

Winter

Growth slows, so water less, stop feeding, and give plants the brightest window you have.

Weekly ritual

Ten minutes, once a week

Observe

Look under the leaves for pests and note any yellowing or drooping.

Feel

Check the soil with your finger before reaching for the watering can.

Adjust

Rotate the pot for even light, remove dead leaves, and move it if the light has changed.
