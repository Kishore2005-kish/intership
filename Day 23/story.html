Since 1974

The story of our coffee

Forty acres under silver oak in Mudigere, a hand-cranked roaster in a Malleswaram garage, and a stubborn belief that Indian coffee belongs on the world's best tables.

The beginning

A hillside, a monsoon, a family

In 1974 our grandfather Venkatesh Iyer traded his job at the Coffee Board for forty acres of neglected land above Mudigere. The first three harvests were lost to leaf rust. The fourth paid for a second-hand pulper, and the fifth for the roastery.

Fifty-two years later the pulper still runs. So does the habit of walking every block before the picking season and rejecting anything that isn't a full, deep-red cherry.

How we work

Three promises we don't bend

01

Fair, fixed prices

Growers are paid a floor price agreed before the season, not after the auction. In a bad year we absorb it.

02

Shade, always

No sun-grown lots. Canopy farming keeps the hills forested, the birds around and the cherry ripening slowly.

03

Nine-day rule

Green coffee is roasted within nine days of milling and leaves the roastery within seven days of roasting.

The people

Two hundred hands before the cup

Pickers in Mudigere, pulpers in Suntikoppa, cooperative graders in Araku, three roasters in Malleswaram and forty-one baristas across six cities. Every bag carries the estate name and the roast date because those people should be on the label.
